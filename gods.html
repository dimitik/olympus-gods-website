The halls of the peak

The twelve who were said to live on the highest mountain.

Hades drew the unseen world under the earth and is not on this list. The nine Muses and the three Graces have desks in the annex. Zeus and Hera run the halls. The rest are here — each with a job Homer and Hesiod would still recognize.

Father of gods and men

Zeus

Son of Rhea and Cronus. Hesiod has Rhea give birth on Mount Dikte in Crete, after a night flight to Lyctos, while the young Kouretes danced the pyrrhic and beat their shields so the father would not hear the crying. Plato’s Cratylus splits the old name into Zeus and Zen: put them back together and you get the cause of living. Diodorus adds that people called him Zen because he ripens the crops with the right weather. As Xenios he keeps guest-law. He married Hera near Knossos, by the river Thereno, and from Olympus he was thought to run rain, thunder, and the living world.

Abilities

Justice and weather. Hospitality as Xenios. A horn of plenty from the goat Amaltheia who raised him, and an aegis cut from her hide — the weapon that won him the Titan war.

Queen, sister, wife

Hera

Sister and wife of Zeus, protector of marriage, honored as Hera Teleia — Hera of the completed vow. One story says she would not yield until a winter day when a frozen cuckoo appeared at her window; she warmed it, and the bird became the god who promised her a lawful wedding. Diodorus places that wedding near Knossos, by the river Thereno, where locals later restaged the rite every year. Pausanias saw a statue of her there by Daedalus. Their three children are Hebe, Ares, and the smith Hephaestus.

Abilities

Marriage, from first vow to finished rite. Before anyone else is honored at a wedding, the sacrifice goes to Zeus Teleios and Hera Teleia — the pair credited with inventing the whole arrangement.

Lord of the sea

Poseidon

Brother of Zeus. After the Titan war the three brothers split the world: sky for Zeus, the unseen downstairs for Hades, water for Poseidon. The Cretans told Diodorus he was first to take up naval work and first to assemble fleets — which is why later generations made him master of everything that happens at sea. They also say he first tamed horses and taught riding, and that is why they called him Hippios. Herodotus thought the name itself was Pelasgian, and that the Egyptians learned this god from the Libyans.

Abilities

Fleets and horses. Lord of the sea, and of the earthquake that goes with it. Sailors still owed him the sacrifice.

Lady of the harvest

Demeter

Sister of Zeus. Wheat used to grow among the weeds and nobody knew what to do with it. She first gathered it, invented processing and storage, and taught sowing — so grain itself took her name, demetriaka. As Thesmophoros she also handed out laws. She had already found wheat before she bore Persephone; when Pluto took the girl she burned the crop. After they made peace she gave the seed to Triptolemus and told him to share it. Even the Athenians, who claimed the discovery, admitted the grain arrived from elsewhere: they called the place Eleusis for that reason.

Abilities

Crops, storage, and the laws that let people live off them. Festivals in her honor ran far beyond Greece, anywhere the bread did.

Wisdom and useful work

Athena

Daughter of Zeus and Metis. Goddess of wisdom and mind. The Cretans call her Tritogeneia, born by the springs of the river Triton, where they still pointed to a temple on the spot. As Ergane she made a wild tree into a crop — the olive was already there, they say, but the care and the oil are hers — plus cloth, carpentry, flutes, and every craft that needs a plan. In the Titan war she is remembered fighting like a man beside her father.

Abilities

The tame olive, the workshop, and any skill that has to be built. Ergane means she works. Tritogeneia means she has a birthplace, not just a helmet.

Lyre, bow, and oracles

Apollo

Son of Zeus and Leto, twin of Artemis. The Cretans name him inventor of the cithara and its music, of medicine that arrives through prophecy, and of the bow — he taught the islanders archery, which is why they still call the longbow Cretan. With Coronis he fathered Asclepius, who took that medicine further into surgery, drugs, and roots. He lingered at Delos, Lycia, and Delphi, so he is also Delios, Lykeios, and Pythios, even though the Cretan story has him born on the island. The Homeric hymn to him says Cretans founded the Delphic brotherhood.

Abilities

A lyre that heals, an oracle that answers in riddles, and the Cretan bow. After he beat Marsyas and punished him too hard, he broke the strings and quit music for a while.

Hunt and the young

Artemis

Daughter of Zeus and Leto, twin of Apollo. Goddess of the countryside and the hunt. They also call her Kourotrophos: she found how to treat and feed infants. She appeared longest at Ephesus, on the Pontus, in Persia, and in Crete, so she is Ephesia, Taurpolos, Persis, and Kresia — same goddess, long tour. Diodorus, following the Cretans, says both twins were born on the island. She hunted with Britomartis, also called Diktynna, who invented hunting nets; some people simply folded the two names into one.

Abilities

The hunt, the wild places, and the care of the very young. Kourotrophos is not a side job. It is how a hunting goddess also kept the next generation alive.

First armor, first tactics

Ares

Son of Zeus and Hera. The myth says he first made a panoply, first armed soldiers, and first taught how a battle is supposed to go — then went after anyone who would not listen to the gods. At Olympia, one story has Apollo beating him at boxing. Bronze is still most of what people remember. The rest is the idea that war, like farming or smithing, had an inventor.

Abilities

Armor, equipment, and the order of a fight. He is not just noise. In this telling he is the first person who treated battle as a craft.

Youth, beauty, marriage season

Aphrodite

Foam-born, and in the usual list a daughter of Cronus; the Cretans also count her among the children of Zeus. She keeps the years when people marry, plus youth and beauty, and the watch over everything that belongs to a wedding. She lingered at Eryx in Sicily, on Kythera, at Paphos in Cyprus, and in Syria, so the locals claimed her as Erykine, Kythereia, Paphia, and Syria. Before her rites, everyone first sacrificed to Zeus Teleios and Hera Teleia — the pair who invented marriage itself.

Abilities

The marrying age, good looks, and the rules of a wedding. Foam at the start, a travel itinerary after that, and a cult in every harbor that wanted her to stay.

Fire and every metal

Hephaestus

Son of Zeus and Hera. He found every work of the forge — bronze, gold, silver, and anything else that will take a fire — and taught the rest of us to use the flame. Smiths prayed and sacrificed to him more than to anyone else. People still called fire itself Hephaestus, so the benefit would not be forgotten. On Olympus he is the only one with a real shop.

Abilities

Every metal that will melt, and fire under its own name. If the others break it, this is the desk it comes back to.

Herald, trade, and the caduceus

Hermes

Son of Zeus and Maia. Herald of the gods — the name is from clear messages, not from inventing words. He first sent embassies in wartime, for peace, truce, and talking things over, and he carries the caduceus that lets a messenger walk through both camps. The benefits are common to both sides, so they named him Common Hermes. He also invented weights, measures, and profit by trade, plus the quieter art of leaving with someone else’s goods unnoticed. After Apollo smashed his cithara, Hermes built a lyre from a tortoise shell and opened the first wrestling ground.

Abilities

The caduceus, same-day news, and a fair set of weights. Also wrestling, a homemade lyre, and — if we are being honest — your missing sandals.

Vine, wine, and stored fruit

Dionysus

In the mainland story he is the son of Zeus and the mortal Semele, and not one of the twelve principals — that seat often belongs to Hestia. The Cretans told Diodorus another birth: Zeus and Persephone, on their island. Orpheus kept a rite in which the Titans tore him to pieces; the same writer notes there were several Dionysuses. What they agree on is the job. He invented the vine and how to grow it, winemaking, and the storing of autumn fruit so people could eat long after the harvest. Near Crete, at the so-called Twin Gulfs, two islands were named the Dionysiad after him — a local proof, they said, that he started there.

Abilities

The vine, the wine, and fruit that lasts the winter. Not always on the official twelve. Always on the table once the grapes are in.

Olympus

The highest mountain in Greece — and, in the old religion, the upstairs hall of the twelve. One god for each month, still looking down on the living world.

How to get here

Olympus stands between Thessaly and Macedonia. The living climb from Litochoro; the myths put the twelve in the halls above the snow, with Zeus and Hera in charge and the Muses and Graces in the annex. Bring decent shoes. The mountain does not issue tickets, and Hades keeps a different door.
