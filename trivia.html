Things worth keeping

Trivia the old writers actually wrote down.

Twelve short notes from Homer, Hesiod, Herodotus, Plato, Diodorus, and Pausanias. Informal on purpose. Useful at a table, or on the path up from Litochoro.

01 / The count

Twelve gods, twelve months, twelve hours.

The main gods were lined up with the twelve months and the twelve hours. Homer and Hesiod handed out the titles and the jobs. The Romans copied the chart. Pindar already speaks of the twelve lords; Athens kept an altar of the Twelve in the agora. If your year has twelve slices, so does the mountain.

02 / The empty chair

Hestia has the hearth. Dionysus has the vine.

The usual twelve include Hestia, sister of Zeus, who invented house-building — almost every home kept an altar for her. Dionysus is Zeus’s son by Semele, inventor of the vine and of wine, and in Crete they also made him a child of Persephone. Some lists swap them. We kept both: she owns this card, he owns a portrait downstairs.

03 / The cover

The Kouretes invented the first noise complaint.

Rhea hid the baby Zeus on Mount Dikte in Crete. Whenever he cried, the young Kouretes — the word is just “the youths” — danced the pyrrhic, yelled, and beat their shields so Cronus would not hear. They had already taught people herding, beekeeping, hunting, and living together. Parenting, but make it percussion.

04 / The goat

The aegis started as a thank-you.

Zeus grew up on the milk of the goat Amaltheia, plus ambrosia from white doves and nectar fetched by an eagle. He broke one of her horns and filled it with every good thing — the horn of plenty. When she died he cut his aegis from her hide. That shield won him the Titan war. Later he set the goat and the eagle among the stars.

05 / The split

Three brothers divided the world.

Zeus beat the Titans — Diodorus puts the fight at Knossos, where people still showed the foundations of Rhea’s house — then the three brothers took their shares. Sky for Zeus, sea for Poseidon, the unseen downstairs for Hades. Olympus is the upstairs office. The basement has a different manager.

06 / The civic kit

Athena tamed the olive. Poseidon tamed the horse.

As Ergane she turned a wild tree into a crop, plus cloth, carpentry, and flutes. He is Hippios because he first taught riding and launched fleets. One city later picked the salad. He is still in the group about it. Both gifts, in the Cretan telling, started as human skills before they became divine job titles.

07 / The staff

Hermes is Common Hermes for a reason.

He invented wartime embassies and the caduceus that lets a messenger walk through both camps. The benefits are common to both sides, so they named him Common Hermes. Also weights, trade, wrestling, and the quiet art of leaving with your stuff. The name, they said, is from clear messages — not from inventing language.

08 / The encore

Apollo smashed a lyre. Hermes built another.

The Cretans give Apollo the cithara. After he beat Marsyas and punished him harder than he should have, he broke the strings and quit music for a while. Hermes then made a lyre from a tortoise shell. That is the original “I can fix it” reply, and it is why two gods share the credit for Greek stringed music.

09 / The first race

The first Olympics were a family joke.

Pausanias: the Idaean Dactyls from Crete — one of them called Heracles, not the Theban one — raced for fun and crowned the winner with wild olive. Another version: Zeus wrestled Cronus here. Apollo beat Hermes at running and Ares at boxing. Later people mixed up the two Heracleses. The mountain, and Olympia below it, is also a stadium.

10 / The name

Zeus means the reason you are still alive.

Plato’s Cratylus: the name was one piece, then people split it into Zeus and Zen. Put them back together and you get the cause of living. Diodorus adds that they called him Zen because he ripens the crops with the right weather, and Father because he cares for everyone. A weather god with a philosophy minor.

11 / The move upstairs

They left the living and kept the job.

Diodorus, recording the Cretans: gods who had done the world a favor were thought to be the first residents of Olympus after they left human company. People assumed they could still help from the peak. That is why a mountain became an address — the original work-from-home policy, except the home is the highest peak in Greece.

12 / The other brother

Hades is not on the mountain.

He is a brother of Zeus and Poseidon. He drew the unseen world under the earth — Hades means the one you do not see — and first taught burial, funeral, and honors for the dead. That is why he is missing from the twelve. Minos and Rhadamanthys judge who goes where. He still answers “maybe” to reunions.

Olympus

The highest mountain in Greece — and, in the old religion, the upstairs hall of the twelve. One god for each month, still looking down on the living world.

How to get here

Olympus stands between Thessaly and Macedonia. The living climb from Litochoro; the myths put the twelve in the halls above the snow, with Zeus and Hera in charge and the Muses and Graces in the annex. Bring decent shoes. The mountain does not issue tickets, and Hades keeps a different door.
